The History of Classic Board Games, From Ancient Boards to Browser Games

The games on this site feel modern because they're playable instantly in a browser, but the games themselves are old — some of them far older than most people realize.

Checkers: thousands of years in the making

Board games resembling checkers have been traced back to ancient Egypt, with the modern rule set most people know today taking shape in Europe centuries later. It's one of the longest continuously played board games in human history, and the core idea — simple diagonal movement, capture by jumping — has barely changed.

The sliding puzzle's surprisingly recent, chaotic origin

Unlike checkers, the sliding tile puzzle is a relatively modern invention, becoming a massive fad in the United States and Europe in the 1870s. Part of its early popularity came from a mathematical quirk: certain scrambled arrangements are mathematically impossible to solve, which led to genuine public debate — and even a cash prize offer — over configurations that could never be completed.

Sudoku's misleading name

Despite the Japanese name, number-placement puzzles very similar to sudoku actually trace back to 19th-century French newspapers, decades before the puzzle was popularized in Japan and later exploded into a worldwide craze in the 2000s under its now-familiar name.

Word puzzles: as old as written language itself

Wordplay and letter-rearrangement puzzles (anagrams) have appeared in various forms for thousands of years, used historically for both entertainment and, at times, for hiding secret messages in plain text.

Same games, new format

What's changed isn't the core appeal of these games — it's the format. A game that once required a physical board, tiles, or a newspaper page now lives in a browser tab, playable in seconds, but the underlying puzzle is often centuries old.
