Checkers vs Chess: Which Is Easier to Learn?

Checkers and chess share the same board, but almost nothing else about how they're learned. If you're deciding which to pick up first, here's an honest breakdown.

Checkers has one rule set for every piece

Every checkers piece moves the same way — diagonally, one square at a time, until it's crowned a king. That's the entire base rule set. A complete beginner can learn every legal move in checkers in about two minutes, which is a big part of why it's often taught to kids before chess.

Chess has six different pieces, each with its own rules

Chess asks you to learn six distinct movement patterns — pawns, knights, bishops, rooks, queens, and kings — plus special rules like castling and en passant. The rules alone take considerably longer to fully absorb, before strategy even enters the picture.

But "simple rules" doesn't mean "no depth"

This is the part people underestimate. Checkers' simplicity is deceptive — top-level checkers strategy is genuinely deep, involving forced sequences and long-range planning that took decades of serious study to fully map out. Easy to learn and easy to master are two different things, and checkers is proof.

Which one should you actually start with?

If you want to be playing a real, satisfying game within minutes, checkers is the clear starting point — you'll be making meaningful decisions almost immediately instead of spending your first sessions just remembering how pieces move. Chess rewards a longer initial investment, but offers more variety in how a game can unfold once you're past the basics.

Learn the rules in two minutes, then start playing.
