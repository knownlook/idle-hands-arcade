How to Play Checkers: Rules for Complete Beginners

Checkers looks simple from across the room — two colors, one board, pieces that hop over each other. It is simple, but "simple" and "obvious" aren't the same thing. If you've never actually played, here's every rule you need, in order, with nothing assumed.

1. The board and starting setup

Checkers is played on the same 8x8 grid as chess, but only the dark squares are ever used. Each player starts with 12 pieces, placed on the dark squares of the three rows closest to them. The two middle rows stay empty at the start. One player uses light pieces, the other dark, and the darker color traditionally moves first.

2. How pieces move

Regular pieces move one square diagonally forward only — never backward, never sideways. "Forward" means toward your opponent's side of the board. This is the single most common rule new players forget: a regular piece can't retreat, even to escape danger.

3. How to capture (jumping)

If an opponent's piece sits diagonally next to yours, and the square directly beyond it is empty, you can jump over it and land on that empty square — removing their piece from the board. Jumps are diagonal only, same as regular moves. If landing your jump sets up another jump immediately, most rule sets require you to take it in the same turn, chaining captures together.

4. Getting kinged

If one of your pieces reaches the very last row on the opponent's side of the board, it gets "kinged" — usually marked by stacking a second piece on top of it. A king can move and capture diagonally in both directions, forward and backward, which makes it far more dangerous than a regular piece.

5. How you actually win

You win by leaving your opponent with no legal move on their turn — either because every one of their pieces has been captured, or because all their remaining pieces are completely blocked in. Games can end faster than they look like they should once one side loses their mobility, even with pieces still on the board.
